Employment and income verification letter: drafted for your employer to sign

Landlords, lenders, visa officers and daycare centres ask for a letter on employer letterhead confirming your job and pay. Most HR teams will sign a correct draft faster than they'll write one. Fill in the facts; the letter states them plainly, adds the annual rate and an estimated take-home from the 2026 tables, and leaves the signature line for the employer. Clean PDF $4.99.

This tool is for the employer, payer or individual whose own records these are. It computes the document from figures you supply and invents nothing. Presenting a document from this site as evidence of pay you did not receive is fraud, and every figure on it must match your real payroll or payment records. See the terms of use.

Frequently asked

Will a landlord accept this?

Once signed by the employer on letterhead, yes — it is the standard verification-of-employment letter. Unsigned it is only a draft.

Can I write it for a contractor?

Use the client as “employer” with employment type “contract”; better still, pair an invoice history with a bank statement.

Why include the take-home estimate?

Landlords compare rent with net income; giving both saves a round-trip. It is labelled as an estimate from the current tables.
