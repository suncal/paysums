Michigan child support calculator (2026): guideline amounts by income

Michigan calculates child support with the income-shares model, which needs both parents' incomes and a state schedule. This page gives the figure every Michigan worksheet starts from — the paying parent's net income after federal tax, FICA and Michigan income tax — for incomes from $3,000 to $15,000 a month, computed from the 2026 tables, and explains how the formula uses it.

Michigan uses the income-shares model: both parents' incomes are combined, a schedule sets the basic support obligation for that combined income and number of children, and each parent owes their proportional share, adjusted for parenting time, health insurance and child-care costs. The schedule is a table, so there is no honest one-parent percentage — use Michigan's official guideline calculator or worksheet with both incomes. What most worksheets start from is each parent's income after tax, which is what the table above gives for Michigan.

What can change the guideline amount

  • Other children the paying parent supports (most states reduce the percentage or the obligation).
  • Shared or split custody — every state has its own adjustment or a separate formula above a parenting-time threshold.
  • Health insurance premiums and child-care costs for the children, usually added and split.
  • Income above the guideline maximum, imputed income when a parent is under-employed, and agreed deviations the court approves.

Frequently asked

How is child support calculated in Michigan?

With the income-shares model: combined parental income against Michigan's schedule, split pro rata, then adjusted for custody time, insurance and child care.

What is child support for one child in Michigan on $5,000 a month?

It depends on the other parent's income too. The paying parent's net income on $5,000 gross is about $4,008 a month in Michigan; the schedule then applies to the combined figure.

Is this legal advice?

No — an estimate for planning. Courts can deviate from the guideline; use the state's official worksheet for anything you file.
