Illinois pay stub requirements (2026)

Required by state law? Yes — new since 1 January 2025

Illinois only began requiring pay stubs on 1 January 2025, and the new duty goes further than most: the employer must keep every stub for three years whether or not the person still works there, and must hand copies over on request — to former employees as well.

The duty, and when it started

Public Act 103-0953 amended the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act (820 ILCS 115/10). The Illinois Department of Labor states the duty plainly: “Starting January 1, 2025, employers in Illinois will be required to provide pay stubs to employees each pay period”, containing “information on hours worked, pay rates, overtime pay, and deductions from wages”.

As enacted, a pay stub under the Act is an itemised statement showing the employee's hours worked, rate of pay, overtime pay, overtime hours worked, gross wages earned, deductions from wages, and total wages and deductions year to date. The year-to-date requirement is the one that trips up spreadsheet payroll — a stub showing only the current period is not sufficient.

Three years of retention, and a right to copies

This is where Illinois goes beyond most states. In IDOL's own words: “Employers must keep a copy of an employee's pay stub for a period of three years from the date of payment, regardless of whether that person remains employed at the business”, and “an employee or former employee may request copies of their paystubs at least twice in a 12-month period.”

As enacted, the employer must furnish the copies within 21 calendar days of the request, and is not obliged to grant more than two requests in any 12 months. An employer who fails to furnish a pay stub as the Act requires is subject to a civil penalty of up to $500 per violation payable to the Department.

The practical effect is that an Illinois employee who has left a job retains a route to the record for three years — which is unusual, and worth knowing if you need pay history for a mortgage or a benefits claim.

What an Illinois pay stub actually shows

Illinois taxes income at a flat 4.95% rather than on a bracket schedule, so the state tax line is simply 4.95% of Illinois taxable income — which makes an Illinois stub unusually easy to check by hand. The state allows a personal exemption of $2,925 (single) or $5,850 (married filing jointly) rather than a standard deduction, applied before the rate. There is no state disability or paid-leave payroll deduction of the kind New York and Washington impose, so beyond federal tax and FICA the Illinois stub is short. Questions about Illinois pay stubs

When did Illinois start requiring pay stubs?

1 January 2025, under Public Act 103-0953 amending the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act. Before that date Illinois had no general pay stub mandate, which is why guidance written earlier says otherwise.

What has to be on an Illinois pay stub?

Hours worked, rate of pay, overtime pay, overtime hours worked, gross wages earned, deductions from wages, and total wages and deductions year to date. The year-to-date figures are part of the requirement, not optional.

Can I get pay stubs from a former Illinois employer?

Yes. The employer must keep each stub for three years from the date of payment regardless of whether you still work there, and a former employee may request copies at least twice in a 12-month period, to be furnished within 21 calendar days.

What is the penalty if an Illinois employer does not provide pay stubs?

A civil penalty of up to $500 per violation, payable to the Illinois Department of Labor. That is separate from any wage claim for money actually owed.

Why is Illinois state tax the same percentage on my whole paycheck?

Illinois uses a flat rate of 4.95% rather than brackets, applied after a personal exemption of $2,925 single or $5,850 married filing jointly. So unlike most states the rate does not change as income rises.
