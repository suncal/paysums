What Is a Paycheck Calculator?

Definition

A paycheck calculator is an online tool that estimates the amount of money you actually receive in each pay period after all mandatory withholdings—federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state income tax, and any applicable city or local taxes—are subtracted from your gross earnings. Paysums.com offers a free, browser‑only paycheck calculator for all 50 U.S. states in 2026, with no account required.

How It Works

Paysums.com runs the calculations locally in your browser, so your salary or hourly rate never leaves your computer. The engine follows the same formulas the IRS and state tax agencies use:

  1. Gross Pay – entered as an annual salary, hourly wage, or a combination of base pay, bonuses, and overtime.
  2. Federal Withholding – based on the 2026 IRS tax brackets, filing status, and the optional standard deduction or itemized amounts you provide.
  3. FICA – 6.2 % for Social Security (up to the 2026 wage base limit) and 1.45 % for Medicare, plus an additional 0.9 % Medicare surtax for wages over $200,000.
  4. State Income Tax – each state’s 2026 tax tables are stored in the app; the calculator applies the correct marginal rates and any state‑specific deductions.
  5. City/Local Tax – for jurisdictions that levy a local income tax (e.g., New York City), the relevant rate is added.
  6. Other Withholdings – you can optionally add pre‑tax deductions such as health insurance, retirement contributions, or flexible‑spending accounts, which reduce taxable wages.

The result is a clear breakdown: gross pay, total taxes, and net (take‑home) pay per pay period (weekly, bi‑weekly, semi‑monthly, or monthly).

Numbers & Examples

Example 1 – Salary in California

  • Gross salary: $75,000
  • Filing status: Single, standard deduction
  • Pay frequency: Bi‑weekly

Calculation

  • Federal tax (2026 brackets, $16,100 standard deduction): $7,670 annually
  • FICA: $5,737.50 (Social Security $4,650 + Medicare $1,087.50)
  • California state income tax and SDI combined: $3,749.50
  • No city tax

Take‑home: $75,000 – $7,670 – $5,737.50 – $3,749.50 = $57,843 per year, or about $2,225 per bi‑weekly paycheck.

Example 2 – Hourly in Texas

  • Hourly rate: $25
  • Hours per week: 40 (full‑time)
  • Annual gross: $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000
  • Filing status: Married filing jointly, standard deduction
  • Pay frequency: Monthly

Calculation

  • Federal tax: $1,980 (($52,000 – $32,200 standard deduction) × 10%)
  • FICA: $3,978 (Social Security $3,224 + Medicare $754)
  • Texas state tax: $0 (no state income tax)
  • No city tax

Take‑home: $52,000 – $1,980 – $3,978 = $46,042 per year, or about $3,837 per month.

These examples illustrate how the same tool can handle different states, filing statuses, and pay structures without any manual spreadsheet work.

FAQ

How accurate is the Paysums.com calculator?

The calculator uses 2026 IRS and state tax rules and the supported city rates, and gives an estimate rather than an exact figure. Tax credits, itemized deductions, and older W‑4 forms are not modelled, so your actual withholding may differ.

Can I save my calculations for later?

All data stays in your browser session. You can copy the results, and the site does not store any personal information. The separate pay stub generator lets you preview a stub for free; downloading the PDF costs $7.99.

Does the tool handle multiple pay periods per year?

Yes. You can select weekly, bi‑weekly, semi‑monthly, or monthly frequencies, and the calculator will prorate taxes accordingly.

What if my state has a local city tax?

Paysums.com includes the major city taxes that levy an income tax (e.g., New York City). If your city is not listed, you can manually add a custom local tax rate.

Is there a limit on how many calculations I can run?

No. The paycheck calculator is free to use as often as you like. The embeddable widget is free with a Powered‑by link; removing the link requires the paid white‑label plan ($19/month or $149/year).
