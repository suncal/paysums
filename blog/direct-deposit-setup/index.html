Direct Deposit Setup: Payroll & Federal Enrollment, Verify First Pay

Published September 17, 2026 · Reviewed against the 2026 tax tables · How we calculate

Gather your bank’s 9-digit routing number and your account number, then either submit your employer’s direct deposit form (paper or portal) or enroll through GoDirect if you’re setting up federal benefits. Payroll deposits usually activate within a few pay cycles; federal benefit enrollments can take several weeks. Setup itself is free in nearly every case.

TL;DR:

  • Accurate entry of routing and account numbers is crucial, as transposed digits are the main cause of rejected deposits or delays.
  • Federal benefit direct deposits require enrollment at least 10 business days before the next payment to avoid delays into the following month.
  • State and local benefit payments often need individual enrollment through specific agency portals or paper forms, not a unified system like federal programs.
  • Keep copies of your enrollment confirmation and turn on deposit alerts to quickly address any missing or delayed deposits.
  • Setting up direct deposit is free, but verifying the deposited amount with a paycheck calculator helps ensure accuracy once the transfer begins.

Step-By-Step Direct Deposit Setup for Payroll

Most employers handle this through HR or a payroll portal like ADP, Gusto, or Workday, though smaller companies may still use a paper form. Either way, the process follows the same basic sequence.

  1. Get the form or portal link. Ask HR for the direct deposit enrollment form, or log into your employer’s payroll portal if one is available.
  2. Fill in your identifying details. This includes your full legal name and, in many cases, your Social Security number or internal employee ID.
  3. Enter your banking information. You’ll need your bank’s routing number and your personal account number, plus a selection for checking or savings.
  4. Attach a voided check if requested. Some employers still require this as a backup verification method, especially for paper enrollment.
  5. Submit and confirm. Return the form to HR or payroll, then follow up to confirm it was received and processed before your next pay date.

A few habits make this go faster:

  • Double-check every digit on your routing and account numbers before submitting. A single transposed digit is the most common cause of a rejected deposit.
  • Ask whether the first deposit will be a live check as a test run. Some payroll systems default to this for the first cycle.
  • Keep a screenshot or copy of your submitted form or portal confirmation. If a deposit goes missing later, this is the first thing HR will ask for.

Payroll providers pull your account details directly into the ACH network using the routing and account numbers you provide, so accuracy at this stage matters more than speed.

What Banking Details and Documents You Need

Before you fill out any form, collect these pieces of information so you’re not stopping mid-enrollment to dig through a drawer:

  • Routing number: a 9-digit code (also called an ABA number) found on the bottom left of a check or inside your bank’s mobile app.
  • Account number: printed to the right of the routing number on checks, or listed under account details online.
  • Account type: checking or savings. Joint accounts work fine, but list the account holder’s name exactly as it appears on the account.
  • Voided check: write “VOID” across a blank check in ink if your employer requests one. Never send a live, signed check.
  • Optional extras: some employers ask for your employee ID or the check number from a recent paycheck to match records.

Pro Tip: If you don’t have paper checks, most banks let you generate a digital voided check image or a direct deposit form straight from their mobile app. Look under “account details” or “documents” before assuming you need to order checks.

How to Enroll for Federal Benefit Direct Deposit

Federal benefits like Social Security, SSI, and VA payments follow a different enrollment path than payroll, and the stakes are higher: paper checks stopped for most federal benefits after September 30, 2025, with only limited waiver exceptions, according to the Social Security Administration.

You have four main enrollment channels:

  • GoDirect online: the fastest option, requiring your Social Security number and either a claim number or your most recent payment amount.
  • My Social Security account: lets you manage direct deposit details directly if you already receive Social Security benefits.
  • Treasury Electronic Payment Solution Center by phone: useful if you’d rather speak with someone or don’t have reliable internet access.
  • FS Form 1199A or FS Form 1200 by mail: the paper route, still accepted but slower than automated ENR enrollment.

If you don’t have a bank account, Direct Express® provides a prepaid debit card alternative that satisfies the electronic payment requirement without a traditional bank.

When to Expect Your First Deposit and What to Do If It’s Missing

Payroll direct deposit typically activates within one pay cycle, though some employers run a test deposit first, which can push it to two cycles. Federal benefits move slower: enrollments accepted at least 10 business days before your next scheduled payment usually mean the following month’s payment arrives by direct deposit. Miss that window and you may wait an extra cycle.

Common causes of a delayed or missing deposit include:

  1. A wrong or transposed routing or account number.
  2. Employer or payroll processing lag, especially right after enrollment.
  3. A temporary hold or pending transaction on the bank’s side.

If a deposit doesn’t show up when expected, work through this order:

  1. Check your bank account activity first. Pending ACH transactions sometimes show up a day before the posted date.
  2. Confirm with payroll or HR that the deposit was actually submitted on their end.
  3. Contact your bank directly if the first two steps don’t explain it. Banks can research a pending or returned transaction faster than most employers can.

Pro Tip: Have your enrollment confirmation, the expected deposit amount, and the date you submitted the form ready before you call. It cuts research time significantly on the bank’s end.

Is Direct Deposit Setup Free, and How Do You Keep It Secure?

Direct deposit is free for recipients in almost all cases. Employers and agencies absorb the processing cost, not you. That said, the setup process handles sensitive account data, so a few precautions matter.

  • Submit forms through secure HR portals rather than email whenever one is available.
  • Never send a photo of a live, signed check. A voided check with no signature is the only version that should leave your hands.
  • Turn on deposit alerts through your bank’s app so you know the moment funds land.

Mistakes that cause delays include entering an old account number from a closed account, transposing digits under the routing number, and forgetting to specify checking versus savings. If you ever suspect fraud, such as a form intercepted in the mail or an account number exposed elsewhere, contact your bank immediately to freeze the affected account and request a new one.

Setting Up Direct Deposit for State and Local Benefits

State unemployment insurance, state disability payments, and local government benefits (property tax rebates, city pension payments) run on separate systems from federal programs. Each state’s labor or treasury department manages its own enrollment, which means the process, forms, and vendor systems differ from state to state and even from one benefit type to another.

Most state unemployment agencies let you choose direct deposit during your initial claim application, entering your routing and account number directly into the online claims portal. Some states default to a prepaid debit card unless you actively select direct deposit, so check that setting carefully if you’d rather have funds land in your own bank account.

For state pension or retirement systems, enrollment usually goes through the pension administrator’s member portal rather than a general treasury site. Local government payments, such as a municipal employee paycheck or a county benefit, typically follow whatever process that specific government office has set up, which might mean a paper form submitted to a payroll clerk rather than an online system.

The key difference from federal enrollment: there’s no single national portal like GoDirect for state and local payments. You’ll need to identify the correct agency for each specific benefit and follow its individual enrollment steps, which usually appear on that agency’s official website under “payment options” or “direct deposit.”

A Practical Habit Worth Building

Setting up direct deposit is a five-minute task that pays off for years, but the part people skip is recordkeeping. Save your enrollment confirmation, whether it’s an email, a portal screenshot, or a stamped copy of a mailed form, somewhere you can find it in six months. Turn on your bank’s deposit alerts too. Once your first deposit lands, running the amount through a take-home pay calculator confirms it matches what you expected after taxes and deductions.

FAQ

Can You Set Up Direct Deposit by Yourself?

Yes. For payroll, you fill out your employer’s direct deposit form or portal yourself using your routing and account numbers. For federal benefits, you can enroll independently through GoDirect or a my Social Security account without needing anyone else’s help.

How Do I Set Up Direct Deposit Online?

For payroll, log into your employer’s payroll portal and enter your bank routing and account numbers under the direct deposit section. For federal benefits, GoDirect’s website walks you through entering your Social Security number and either a claim number or your last payment amount.

How Much Does It Cost to Set Up Direct Deposit?

Direct deposit setup is free for recipients in nearly every case, whether you’re enrolling through an employer or a federal agency. If you want to verify your expected net pay once deposits begin, Paysums’ take-home pay calculator is also free to use.

How Long Does Direct Deposit Take to Set Up?

Payroll direct deposit typically activates within one to two pay cycles after you submit your form. Federal benefit enrollments accepted at least 10 business days before your next scheduled payment usually take effect the following month.
