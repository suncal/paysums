Instant IRS Transcripts and Pay Stubs for Employees and Contractors

Published September 26, 2026 · Reviewed against the 2026 tax tables · How we calculate

The fastest way to get a pay stub is to download it from your employer’s payroll portal or request a copy from HR. If neither works, or you no longer work there, an IRS transcript or Social Security earnings record serves as an official substitute. Contractors rely on 1099s, invoices, and bank records instead of stubs, and a clearly labeled self-generated stub is a documented last resort, not a first move.

TL;DR:

  • Download pay stubs directly from payroll portals like ADP or Gusto, as they provide the fastest and most credible proof of income for current employees.
  • When unavailable, IRS transcripts covering the last 10 years serve as a reliable official substitute for income verification.
  • Contractors should rely on a combination of 1099-NEC forms, invoices, and bank deposit records to establish consistent income.
  • A self-generated pay stub is acceptable only as a last resort, with careful attention to accuracy and supporting documentation.
  • Always verify figures with a paycheck calculator before creating a self-produced document to ensure correctness and compliance.

Shortlist: fastest ways to get a verified pay stub

Your best option depends on your employment status and how recently you worked for the employer. Current employees usually have the quickest path; former employees and contractors need a different route entirely.

  • Portal download: if your employer uses a payroll system like ADP, QuickBooks, or Gusto, your stub is available online within minutes.
  • Ask HR or payroll: most employers will email or print a copy at no cost, though turnaround varies by company.
  • Payroll provider support line: useful when your employer’s HR team is small or the portal itself is down.
  • IRS transcript or SSA earnings record: the standard fallback for former employees, missing W-2s, or employers that no longer operate.
  • Pay-stub generator or DIY stub: appropriate only when official documentation genuinely cannot be obtained in time, and only when the figures are accurate and clearly labeled.

Each option carries a different tradeoff between speed, cost, and how much weight a lender or landlord will give the document. A portal download or HR copy is the gold standard because it comes straight from payroll records. Government transcripts rank a close second in credibility. A self-generated stub sits lowest on that scale and should be paired with supporting evidence, covered later in this article.

How to get pay stubs from payroll portals and HR (step-by-step)

Most employers set up portal access during onboarding, so check your inbox for the original invitation before assuming you need to start from scratch.

  1. Search your e-mail for the payroll provider’s name (ADP, Paychex, Gusto, or similar) and follow the sign-up or password-reset link.
  2. If you never received an invitation, ask your manager or HR to resend one, or request temporary access directly.
  3. Once logged in, go to the pay statements or pay history section and select the pay period you need.
  4. Download each stub as a PDF rather than taking a screenshot, since lenders often expect a full, unedited file.
  5. Save the file with a clear name, such as the pay date and employer, and store a backup copy outside the portal in case access ends later.

If HR needs to send a copy directly, keep the request simple: “Could you send me PDF copies of my pay stubs for [dates]? I need them for [loan, apartment, tax] purposes.” Most payroll departments can fulfill this within a few business days.

When you open a stub, check the gross pay, net pay, year-to-date (YTD) totals, and itemized deductions for taxes, retirement, and benefits. Lenders typically scan the YTD figures first to confirm consistent income over time.

Pro Tip: Download every stub the day it posts. Portal access is usually cut off shortly after you leave a job, and re-requesting old statements later takes far longer than saving them as you go.

If your employer won’t or can’t provide pay stubs (official alternatives)

Federal law does not actually require employers to issue pay stubs at all. The Fair Labor Standards Act requires employers to keep accurate payroll records, but it stops short of mandating that those records be handed to employees in stub form. That gap is why government alternatives matter so much when an employer is unresponsive, defunct, or simply doesn’t distribute stubs.

  • IRS tax transcript: free, available immediately online, and covers up to the last 10 tax years, making it a strong substitute for income verification.
  • Replacement W-2: request it from your employer first; if that fails, the IRS can help, though a full copy of your tax return ordered through Form 4506 carries a fee and a longer wait than a transcript.
  • SSA earnings statement: shows wages reported by current and former employers, useful when you need a longer employment history rather than a single pay period.
  • State labor department or DOL complaint: worth contacting if an employer is withholding payroll records it’s legally required to keep under the DOL’s recordkeeping rules.

IRS transcripts are free and download instantly online, covering up to the last 10 tax years, which makes them one of the fastest official documents available when a pay stub simply doesn’t exist anymore. A full copy of a tax return through Form 4506, by contrast, involves a fee and a slower processing time, so reach for the transcript first unless a lender specifically asks for the return itself.

What contractors should use instead of pay stubs (income-proof checklist)

Independent contractors don’t receive pay stubs because they aren’t on anyone’s payroll. Instead, income documentation comes from a different set of records entirely.

  • Form 1099-NEC: payers issue this when they’ve paid a contractor $600 or more in a year, and it’s the closest equivalent to a W-2 for tax purposes.
  • Invoices: dated, itemized invoices show what was billed and when, filling in gaps a 1099 alone can’t cover.
  • Bank deposit records: exported statements confirm that invoiced amounts were actually paid, which lenders often want to see alongside the 1099.
  • Client verification letters: a short letter from a client confirming the working relationship and payment history can strengthen a thin file.

A 1099 by itself rarely satisfies a lender because it only shows annual totals, not the timing or consistency of income. Combining a 1099-NEC with invoices and bank records gives a fuller, more convincing picture. A bank statement converter can speed up the process of turning months of transactions into a clean, exportable format for a loan application or landlord request.

How to generate a pay stub yourself (lawful, accurate method)

A self-generated stub is appropriate when a copy was lost, an employer has closed, or a legitimate delay makes an official copy impossible to get in time. It is never appropriate as a way to inflate or misrepresent actual earnings.

  1. Include every field a lender expects: employer name, employee name, pay period, pay date, hours worked, gross pay, net pay, YTD totals, and itemized deductions.
  2. Run your actual wage and filing details through an up-to-date paycheck calculator to compute accurate federal and state withholding rather than estimating by hand.
  3. Save the calculator output alongside the finished stub so the numbers can be checked against a documented source.
  4. Attach supporting evidence such as bank deposit records, invoices, or an employer e-mail confirming pay.
  5. Label the document clearly, for example “self-generated for verification,” so it’s never mistaken for an official employer record.

Pro Tip: Keep the underlying calculation file, not just the finished PDF. A lender or landlord who asks how a number was reached will trust a documented worksheet far more than a number with no paper trail behind it.

Why official income proof matters more than a polished template

Pay stubs get treated as a formality until someone actually needs one, and that’s usually the moment they discover their employer never distributed one in the first place. The federal government doesn’t require a stub at all, only accurate payroll records kept for a few years, so a lot of the frustration people feel is really a mismatch between what they assumed the law guaranteed and what it actually does.

The more useful shift is to stop treating a pay stub as the goal and start treating income verification as the goal. A transcript, a bank record, an SSA earnings statement, and a well-labeled self-generated document all serve that same purpose with different levels of authority behind them. Readers who understand that hierarchy waste far less time chasing the wrong document for their situation.

FAQ

What can I use if I don’t have a pay stub?

An IRS tax transcript, an SSA earnings statement, or a recent bank statement showing direct deposits can all substitute for a pay stub. For contractors, a 1099-NEC combined with invoices and bank records usually works just as well.

How do I get a pay stub if I have direct deposit?

Direct deposit doesn’t eliminate your pay stub. Your employer’s payroll portal still generates one each pay period, so log in and download it, or ask HR for a copy if you can’t access the portal.

How do I generate my own pay stubs?

Use an accurate paycheck calculator to compute your gross pay, withholding, and net pay, then list those figures along with your pay period and YTD totals on a clearly labeled document. Keep the calculator output and any bank records as backup in case someone questions the figures.

Where can I find my pay stubs?

Current employees can usually find them in their employer’s payroll portal, such as ADP or Gusto, under pay history or pay statements. If that portal is no longer accessible, an IRS transcript or a request to HR are the next best sources.
