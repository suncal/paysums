Local Payroll Taxes: Who Withholds Them and How They Work

Published October 19, 2026 · Reviewed against the 2026 tax tables · How we calculate

Local payroll taxes are income taxes that cities, townships, school districts or counties charge on wages, in addition to federal and state withholding. Most states have none. Where they exist, as in Pennsylvania, Ohio and Philadelphia, the employer withholds them from each paycheck, usually based on the work location, the home address, or the higher of the two. The rules differ by state, so the rate on a pay stub often does not match the rate a city publishes.

TL;DR

  • Local payroll taxes are a separate layer on top of federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare and state income tax.
  • Pennsylvania withholds local earned income tax (EIT) at the higher of the work or residence rate. Ohio generally withholds municipal tax by work location, with reciprocity exceptions.
  • Philadelphia’s wage tax is 3.735% for residents and 3.425% for nonresidents from July 1, 2026.
  • Pre-tax 401(k) deferrals lower federal income tax withholding but not Social Security or Medicare tax, and local rules on retirement deferrals vary.
  • Freelancers have no employer withholding, so local tax is settled on a return or through estimated payments.

What counts as a local payroll tax

Federal payroll taxes are uniform. In 2026 the employee pays 6.2% Social Security on wages up to $184,500 and 1.45% Medicare on all wages, plus 0.9% Additional Medicare tax above $200,000. The employer pays a matching 6.2% and 1.45%.

Local taxes are not uniform. They are set by the locality or by state law, and they typically fall into these groups:

Type What it taxes Example
City wage or earnings tax Wages earned in or by residents of a city Philadelphia Wage Tax
Earned income tax (EIT) Wages and net self-employment income Pennsylvania municipalities and school districts
Municipal income tax Wages earned in the municipality, and resident income Ohio cities and villages
School district income tax Resident income, in some districts Ohio and Pennsylvania
Flat per-worker levy A fixed dollar amount per year Pennsylvania Local Services Tax (LST)

The tax is withheld by the employer and remitted to a local collector. The employee then reconciles it on a local or state return. Employers that cannot track these rules accurately tend to make errors, which is why local tax tables belong in any payroll tool.

Where the rate comes from: work location or home address

The central question for every local tax is which place gets the money. There are three common answers.

  1. Work location. The city where the employee performs the work taxes the wages. This is the default in Ohio.
  2. Residence. The home jurisdiction taxes the wages wherever they are earned. Credits for tax paid elsewhere are common.
  3. Higher of the two. Pennsylvania uses this approach for EIT.

A remote worker, or an employee with a long commute across a municipal line, can have a different local rate than a coworker in the same office.

Pennsylvania: EIT, LST and the higher-rate rule

Pennsylvania has the most layered local system. Employers should withhold and remit the local earned income tax and local services tax for employees working in the state. Collection is handled by third-party agencies that are assigned by county.

How EIT withholding works

Under Act 32, EIT collection was consolidated so that employers remit to a single tax collector for the collection district. When residence and work localities have different EIT rates, the employer withholds at the higher of the two. If the rates are equal, the withholding goes to the residence locality.

This explains a common surprise. An employee living in a 1% jurisdiction and working in a 2% jurisdiction sees 2% withheld. The extra amount is distributed according to the collection rules, and the employee reconciles with the local collector for the residence jurisdiction. Annual returns for individual taxpayers are due April 15.

Local Services Tax

The LST is a flat annual amount in many municipalities, withheld across pay periods. The cap is $52 per year under the state framework, and many workers pay it. Rules on exemptions for low earners are set locally, so the collector’s notice for the specific municipality controls.

Philadelphia

Philadelphia runs its own Wage Tax. The Department of Revenue set the rates effective July 1, 2026 at 3.735% for residents and 3.425% for nonresidents. These are part of a five-year schedule of reductions, so payroll tables need a new update each July.

For state-level figures and the state-plus-local picture, see the Pennsylvania paycheck calculator.

Ohio: municipal income tax and reciprocity

Ohio municipal income taxes are local levies that can apply to residents and to nonresidents who work within a municipality. The rate is set locally. A municipality may levy up to 1% without voter approval. Higher rates need a vote, which is why rates vary between cities and can change after local ballot measures.

Employers with an office or business activity in Ohio withhold the tax on compensation for personal services performed in the state.

Reciprocity exceptions

Ohio has reciprocity agreements with Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Pennsylvania and West Virginia. For employees living in those states, employers should not withhold Ohio municipal income tax even when the work is performed at an Ohio location. Withholding follows the employee’s resident-state rules.

Mid-year changes

If an employee reports a taxing district in June, the employer is not required to go back to January. It starts withholding school district tax from the point it was notified, per the same Ohio FAQ.

States that look local but are not

Some states are often grouped with local-tax states even though their withholding is statewide. New Jersey is a useful comparison. Its payroll tax withholding follows graduated state rates. Employers use Form NJ-W4 to determine whether Rate A or Rate B applies, and they subtract a pay-period allowance, such as $19.20 weekly, before applying the rate table.

The result is a city-specific pay stub without a city income tax. See the New Jersey paycheck calculator or the example of $40,000 after taxes in Newark, NJ for how the state layer alone works.

Worked example: how a local tax changes a paycheck

The figures below are a simplified illustration, not a payroll result. They assume a $2,000 bi-weekly gross wage, a hypothetical 2% local rate on gross wages, and ignore state tax and deductions.

Line Rate Amount
Gross wages $2,000.00
Social Security 6.2% $124.00
Medicare 1.45% $29.00
Local income tax (hypothetical) 2% $40.00
Subtotal withheld, before federal and state income tax $193.00

Each 1% of local rate costs $20 per bi-weekly paycheck at this wage, or $520 per year over 26 pay periods. Local tax is usually small next to federal tax, but it is a permanent reduction in take-home pay and counts in any job comparison between two cities.

How local tax interacts with deductions

  • 401(k) deferrals. Elective deferrals are excluded from federal income tax withholding but remain subject to Social Security and Medicare tax. Whether a locality taxes the deferred amount depends on its own definition of taxable compensation. Check the local collector’s rules before assuming a deferral lowers the local base.
  • Pay frequency. Flat percentage taxes scale with each paycheck. Per-year flat amounts, such as the LST, are divided over the pay periods.
  • Multiple jurisdictions. A worker with two work locations may owe different local taxes on different portions of pay.

The take-home pay calculator and the bi-weekly paycheck calculator accept pre-tax deductions and pay frequency, which makes the effect of each input visible.

If an injured worker receives wage-replacement benefits, taxability is a separate question from payroll withholding. This reference on whether workers’ compensation is taxable covers the federal rules.

Freelancers and contractors

Employers generally do not withhold taxes from payments to independent contractors. The IRS states that anyone expecting to owe $1,000 or more in taxes typically makes quarterly estimated payments, covering income tax and self-employment tax.

Local tax is a separate matter. Many local earned income taxes apply to net self-employment income, and no one withholds them. A contractor in a local-tax area should add the local rate to the quarterly estimate. The 1099 tax calculator and the W-2 vs. 1099 comparison show the federal side of that estimate.

Checklist for employers

  1. Confirm the employee’s home address and work address at hire and after any move.
  2. Identify the local collector for each jurisdiction. In Pennsylvania this is set by county.
  3. Apply the correct rule: work location, residence, or higher of the two.
  4. Check reciprocity agreements before withholding for out-of-state residents.
  5. Update rates on the effective dates, as with Philadelphia’s July 1 change.
  6. Deposit federal taxes on the IRS employment tax schedule and remit local taxes to the local collector on its own schedule.

Our perspective

We see the same pattern in the questions that reach us: workers compare a published city rate with the line on a pay stub and assume one of them is wrong. Usually both are right. The stub reflects a rule, such as the higher-of-two rate in Pennsylvania or the work-location rule in Ohio, that the city rate page does not describe.

Our position is that local tax should be modeled as an input, not an afterthought. The rate depends on where the work is done, where the employee lives and how the state coordinates the two. We update our tables weekly from IRS, SSA and state sources, and we recommend checking any local result against the collector’s notice for the specific municipality before relying on it for a budget or a job decision.

FAQ

Does Ohio have a local income tax?

Yes. Ohio municipalities can levy an income tax on residents and on nonresidents who work there. A municipality may set a rate of up to 1% without voter approval, and higher rates need a vote. Residents of Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Pennsylvania and West Virginia are covered by reciprocity agreements.

Are local payroll taxes based on work location?

It depends on the state. Ohio generally withholds by work location. Pennsylvania withholds EIT at the higher of the work and residence rates. Always check the rule for the specific state and municipality.

How much is the Philadelphia wage tax in 2026?

From July 1, 2026, the rate is 3.735% for residents and 3.425% for nonresidents, according to the city’s Department of Revenue as reported by a Philadelphia-area accounting firm. The rates are scheduled to fall again in later years.

Do 401(k) contributions reduce local tax?

Not automatically. Federally, deferrals lower income tax withholding but not Social Security or Medicare tax. Local treatment depends on how the locality defines taxable compensation, so check the local collector’s guidance.

Do freelancers pay local income tax?

Often yes, where the locality taxes net self-employment income. No employer withholds it, so it is settled on a local return or built into quarterly estimates.
