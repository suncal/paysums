Nanny payroll calculator 2026: what your nanny takes home and what she costs you, by state

What the family pays on top of wages

  • Social Security and Medicare, employer share: 7.65% of cash wages once the employee reaches $3,000 in the year (then on all of it).
  • FUTA: 0.6% of the first $7,000 = $42 a year, once you pay $1,000 in any quarter.
  • State unemployment insurance: most states require household employers to register and pay a rate on the first few thousand dollars of wages.
  • Reported on Schedule H with your Form 1040; W-2 to the nanny by January 31.

Frequently asked

Do I have to withhold income tax from my nanny's pay?

No — only Social Security and Medicare are mandatory. Income tax withholding is optional and by agreement (the nanny gives you a W-4); most families do it so the nanny doesn't face a bill in April.

What if I pay less than the threshold?

Under $3,000 in cash wages for the year, no Social Security or Medicare is owed on that employee's wages; FUTA still applies if you paid $1,000 or more in a calendar quarter.

Can I pay my nanny as a 1099 contractor?

No. A nanny working in your home on your schedule is an employee under IRS and state rules; issuing a 1099 instead of a W-2 is misclassification and leaves the nanny paying both halves of Social Security.
