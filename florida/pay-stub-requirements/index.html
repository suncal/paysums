Florida pay stub requirements (2026)

Required by state law? No — Florida has no pay stub law at all

Florida is one of a small group of states with no wage-statement statute of any kind. There is no Florida equivalent of Texas Labor Code § 62.003 or California Labor Code § 226 — nothing in Chapter 448 or Chapter 532 requires an employer to tell you how your pay was worked out. The twist is that Florida does require you to state your dates, hours and the exact amount owed before you may sue over unpaid wages, while giving you no right to the record that would prove any of it.

Does Florida require employers to give pay stubs?

No. There is no Florida statute requiring an employer to furnish a pay stub, an earnings statement or a wage statement — not for hourly workers, not for salaried ones, not on request. Chapter 448 (labor) sets no such duty, and Chapter 532 (payment of wages) governs only the instrument wages are paid with, not any accompanying record. Many employers provide stubs anyway, because payroll software produces them and because they need the record themselves — but that is practice, not law.

What does bind every Florida employer is federal: the FLSA requires the employer to make and keep the payroll record — hours worked, rate, straight-time and overtime earnings, every deduction, the dates of the pay period (29 CFR § 516.2, under 29 U.S.C. § 211(c)). The Department of Labor is explicit that this is a duty to keep, not to hand over: “the FLSA does not require an employer to provide employees pay stubs.” So in Florida the record always exists. You simply have no statutory right to see it.

Why that gap matters more in Florida than elsewhere

Before you can sue a Florida employer for unpaid minimum wages, the statute makes you quantify the claim first:

“Prior to bringing any claim for unpaid minimum wages pursuant to this section, the person aggrieved shall notify the employer alleged to have violated this section, in writing, of an intent to initiate such an action.” The notice must state “the minimum wage to which the person aggrieved claims entitlement”, “the actual or estimated work dates and hours for which payment is sought”, and “the total amount of alleged unpaid wages”. The employer then has 15 calendar days to pay or resolve the claim.

Fla. Stat. § 448.110(6)(a)–(b)

Dates, hours and a total — from an employee the state never gave a right to a pay record. That is why keeping your own contemporaneous record in Florida is worth more than it is in a state that mandates statements, and why the word “estimated” is in the statute at all.

Florida's minimum wage reached $15.00 on 30 September 2026

Florida sets its minimum wage in the state constitution rather than by statute, which is why it steps up on 30 September instead of 1 January:

“Effective September 30th, 2021, the existing state Minimum Wage shall increase to $10.00 per hour, and then increase each September 30th thereafter by $1.00 per hour, until the Minimum Wage reaches $15.00 per hour on September 30th, 2026.”

Fla. Const. art. X, § 24(c)

That was the last scheduled step. From 2027 the rate is adjusted every September by CPI-W inflation rather than by a fixed dollar amount. The tipped cash wage moved to $11.98 on the same date — Florida applies a fixed $3.02 tip credit to whatever the minimum wage is, so the two move together.

Enforcement is unusually strong for a state with no wage-statement law. An employee who prevails recovers “the full amount of any back wages unlawfully withheld plus the same amount as liquidated damages, and shall be awarded reasonable attorney's fees and costs”, and it is “unlawful for an Employer or any other party to discriminate in any manner or take adverse action against any person in retaliation for exercising rights protected under this amendment”.

How Florida wages may be paid

“Any order, check, draft, note, memorandum, payroll debit card, or other acknowledgment of indebtedness issued in payment of wages or salary due or to become due must be negotiable and payable in cash, on demand, without discount, at some established place of business in the state”.

Fla. Stat. § 532.01

Payroll debit cards are expressly allowed. What is not allowed is a card or instrument you can only convert at a discount — it must be payable at full value, on demand, at a named Florida place of business, and the employer must keep funds available to cover it for at least 30 days.

Deductions

Florida has no general statute restricting deductions from private-sector wages — nothing comparable to Texas Labor Code § 61.018, which requires written authorisation. The limit is the federal floor: a deduction for the employer's benefit may not take an employee below the minimum wage for the week, and may not cut into overtime pay. Because Florida's minimum wage is now $15.00 rather than the federal $7.25, that floor sits considerably higher here than in most states, and a deduction that would be lawful elsewhere can be unlawful in Florida.

What a Florida pay stub actually shows

Florida levies no personal income tax, so a Florida earnings statement has no state income tax line. What remains is federal income tax under the employee's W-4, Social Security at 6.2% to the wage base, Medicare at 1.45% with the additional 0.9% above $200,000, and any pre-tax deductions. Florida's reemployment tax is paid by the employer and never appears as an employee deduction — if you see it on a stub, something is wrong. Questions about Florida pay stubs

Is it illegal for a Florida employer not to give a pay stub?

No. Florida has no statute requiring a pay stub or wage statement, so an employer who never issues one is not breaking a Florida law. They must still keep the underlying payroll record under federal law, and they must still pay you correctly — the absence of a stub does not reduce what you are owed.

What is the Florida minimum wage right now?

$15.00 an hour, since 30 September 2026 — the final step of the 2020 constitutional amendment. The tipped cash wage is $11.98, because Florida applies a fixed $3.02 tip credit. From September 2027 the rate rises with CPI-W inflation instead of by a set dollar amount.

How do I prove my income in Florida without pay stubs?

Bank deposits are the usual starting point, and for self-employed and 1099 work they are often all there is. A lender or landlord will generally accept deposit history plus a tax return. If you need a formal statement of what you were actually paid, build one from your own records — the figures must match what you really received, and the document should say who prepared it.

Can my Florida employer pay me on a payroll debit card?

Yes. Section 532.01 expressly lists a payroll debit card among the permitted instruments. The condition is that it be “negotiable and payable in cash, on demand, without discount” at a named place of business in Florida — so a card that charges you to access your own wages at full value is the problem, not the card itself.

My Florida employer will not pay me. What do I do?

For unpaid minimum wages, section 448.110 requires you to send written notice first, stating the wage you claim, the dates and hours involved and the total owed. The employer has 15 calendar days to resolve it. If they do not, a successful claim recovers the back wages plus an equal amount again as liquidated damages, plus attorney's fees and costs — and retaliating against you for bringing it is itself unlawful.
