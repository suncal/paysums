Income needed to buy the median home, by state and city (2026)

At this week's 7.32% 30-year rate with 20% down, the median US home ($360,600) costs $2,354 a month with taxes and insurance and needs an income of about $100,904. It ranges from $45,508 in West Virginia to $225,758 in Hawaii; among big cities from $28,839 in Detroit to $361,789 in San Francisco.

How the income needed is calculated

Median home value and median property tax are the Census Bureau's American Community Survey 2024 1-year estimates (tables B25077 and B25103, owner-occupied homes). The payment assumes a 20% down payment, a 30-year fixed loan at 7.32% — the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey average for the week of 2026-10-22, refreshed here every Thursday — plus the median property tax and homeowners insurance at 0.35% of the value a year. Income needed uses the conventional 28% front-end rule: the full payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance) must not exceed 28% of gross income. Take-home pay at that income is computed by the paycheck engine for a single filer with no other deductions (2026 tables), so the last column shows what the payment really takes out of a monthly paycheck. A 10% down payment raises the income needed by roughly 12%; two incomes can share it. The full table is in open data (CSV, CC BY 4.0).

Frequently asked

How much income do I need to buy a house in the US?

About $100,904 a year for the median home ($360,600) at 7.32% with 20% down — a payment of $2,354 a month including property tax and insurance. Half of all homes cost less, and the number falls fast with a lower price or rate.

Which state needs the highest income to buy a home?

Hawaii: the median home is $875,900, which needs about $225,758 a year. West Virginia is the lowest at $45,508.

Why is the payment a bigger share of take-home than 28%?

Because 28% is measured against gross income; after federal, FICA and state tax the same payment is typically 35–45% of what actually lands in the bank, which is why the last column matters more than the rule.
